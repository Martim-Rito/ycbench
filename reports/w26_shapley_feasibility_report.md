# W26 Shapley Regression: feasibility experiment

_Generated by `scripts/run_w26_shapley_feasibility.py`. **Feasibility / methodological experiment, not a result.** Nothing here shows that Shapley Regression is a good startup predictor, that k=2 is optimal, that k=3 is inherently worse, or that any interaction is meaningful._

## 0. Provenance

- YC Bench repo HEAD `892f98d`; official `shapley_regression` clone at `8711f51` (working tree clean after the run: True); scikit-learn 1.9.1, numpy 2.4.3, pandas 3.0.1.
- Modelling uses the official `ChoquisticRegression(representation='shapley', k_add=k, scale_data=True)` unchanged (MinMax scaling -> Shapley basis -> sklearn `LogisticRegression`). Our code only adds the imputer, the manual CV loop, coalition bookkeeping and statistics.
- The official estimator always forwards `penalty=`, which scikit-learn 1.9.1 flags with a FutureWarning (deprecated in 1.8, removed in 1.10). The warning is expected and was not suppressed in the fits; convergence warnings are counted per condition.
- Dataset: `data/processed/w26_dataset_v1.csv`, 198 `target_defined` rows, 20 positives (`target_top20`), unchanged. Google columns are **not** used.

## 1. Design

- Feature sets: **S3** = founder_n_founders, company_one_liner_n_words, founder_previous_yc (n=3). **S5** = S3 + TLD_ai + TLD_other, .com as reference (n=5). TLD is treated as a sensitivity set: its association with the target is plausibly a Google/domain measurement artefact, so TLD coefficients are not interpreted.
- **S4 (extra, not in the brief)** = S3 + one `TLD_not_com` dummy (n=4). Added after the rank diagnostic in section 2b showed that S5's two mutually exclusive TLD dummies create redundant Shapley columns. S4 removes that structural redundancy for k<=2, but section 2c shows S4 k=3 is still rank-deficient for a different, finite-sample reason. **S3 is the only set that is full column rank at k=1,2,3** (k=3 is poorly conditioned), so S3 is the primary set for the k comparison and for the regularisation comparison; S4 and S5 are sensitivity sets.
- Imputation (median for numeric, most frequent for binary; 5 rows, 0 positives) and MinMax scaling are fitted inside each training fold. L2, C=1, lbfgs, max_iter=1000, no class weights, no tuning.
- `RepeatedStratifiedKFold` 5x10, seed 0 (50 fits per condition). Top-20 uses the same tie rule as `run_w26_v1_experiment.py` (OOF predictions per repeat, ties by slug order). Theoretical chance Top-20 = 2.0; the 100-permutation calibration in section 6 shows the pooled OOF Top-20 under shuffled labels is about 1.6 on S3, so both references are given.
- Shuffled-label null: `y` permuted with `numpy.random.default_rng(s).permutation`, s in 1000..1009 (10 permutations, deterministic); stratified CV (seed 0) is re-run on each permuted `y`. Null cells are the mean over permutations (± SD across permutations), so a single lucky shuffle cannot define the null. This is a deliberate deviation from 'a shuffle seed' (one shuffle with 20 positives is too noisy to be a reference).
- Coefficient statistics: |coef| <= 1e-06 is numerically zero. **Sign consistency** = max(fraction positive, fraction negative) among the fits in which |coef| > 1e-06, over the 50 fits (defined before inspecting results). 70% is a descriptive reference level, not a test. Rank stability = mean Spearman correlation between the signed order>=2 coefficient vectors of different folds of the same repeat; training sets overlap by 60-75%, so all stability numbers are optimistic.
- Coalition names come from our own enumeration (`itertools.combinations`, orders 1..k), verified against the official basis (section 2), not from the official `Feature_i` names.

## 2b. Basis rank diagnostic (found while inspecting results)

Rank = rank of the centred Shapley basis (all 198 rows, MinMax-scaled). **Structural rank** = the same on a simulated sample of 100,000 rows with the observed marginals, independent features and mutually exclusive TLD groups, i.e. a sample in which every logically possible feature combination is observed (seeded). Deficiency that is also present in the simulation is **structural** (implied by the encoding); deficiency present only in the observed data is **finite-sample** (some combination is too rare or never observed). Condition number = largest / smallest non-zero singular value of the centred basis (descriptive; a full-rank basis with a large condition number is still weakly identified).

| set | k | #columns | rank (198 rows) | structural rank (simulated) | redundant: structural | redundant: finite-sample only | smallest singular value | condition number |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S3 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 2.193 | 1 |
| S3 | 2 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0.180 | 20 |
| S3 | 3 | 7 | 7 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 0.033 | 113 |
| S5 | 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 2.175 | 3 |
| S5 | 2 | 15 | 13 | 14 | 1 | 1 | 0.170 | 50 |
| S5 | 3 | 25 | 16 | 21 | 4 | 5 | 0.022 | 407 |
| S4 | 1 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 2.178 | 3 |
| S4 | 2 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 0.170 | 47 |
| S4 | 3 | 14 | 12 | 14 | 0 | 2 | 0.021 | 391 |

**S5 (structural).** `TLD_ai` and `TLD_other` are mutually exclusive, so `min(TLD_ai, TLD_other) = 0` and the Shapley column for that pair equals −(TLD_ai + TLD_other)/2 exactly (asserted in section 2): a linear function of the two main-effect columns, not an interaction. Every triple containing both dummies is likewise in the span of lower-order columns. These coefficients are decided by the penalty, not by the data. S5 additionally has finite-sample redundancies because `founder_previous_yc` is 1 in only 5 rows. **S5 is kept as requested but is a sensitivity analysis only; its TLD_ai × TLD_other and TLD-pair triples are artefacts of dummy coding.**

## 2c. Why S4 k=3 is still rank-deficient (mechanism)

S4 = {a = n_founders, w = one-liner words, p = founder_previous_yc, t = TLD_not_com}. The structural rank is full at k=1,2,3 (simulated column), so the encoding itself implies no dependency; the two redundant columns come from the data. Findings (asserted in section 2):

1. `p` and `t` are binary, so min(p,t) = p·t, and the Shapley column φ(p,t) = pt − (p+t)/2. The cell p=1 & t=1 contains **exactly one row** (row index [177]; only 5 rows have p=1, 4 of them with t=0).
2. For binary p,t and any other feature u in [0,1], min(u,p,t) = u·p·t, which is non-zero only in that single row. In that row a and w are fixed numbers a*, w*, so **min(a,p,t) = a*·min(p,t) and min(w,p,t) = w*·min(p,t) on all 198 rows** (asserted, a*=0.333, w*=0.182).
3. Each Shapley column φ(A) is a fixed linear combination of the min-terms min(C), C⊆A, with coefficient 1 on min(A) itself, so the k=3 basis is an invertible (triangular) recombination of the min-term matrix and has the same rank. The min-term matrix contains min(p,t), min(a,p,t), min(w,p,t), which by (2) satisfy two exact linear relations (min(a,p,t) − a*·min(p,t) = 0 and min(w,p,t) − w*·min(p,t) = 0). Hence 2 redundant columns: 14 columns, rank 12. Empirically the null space of the centred basis is 2-dimensional and its sparse basis contains exactly the φ-columns that feed these two relations (e.g. φ(a), φ(a,p), φ(a,t), φ(p,t), φ(a,p,t)).
4. This is **finite-sample**, not structural: the simulation (all combinations observed) is full rank, and a single further row with p=t=1 and different a and w would restore it. It is a rare-support problem of `founder_previous_yc` (5 rows of 198 have p=1, only 1 of them with t=1), not of the encoding of TLD.
5. There is also a near-dependence: the smallest non-zero singular value of S4 k=3 is 0.021 (condition number ≈ 390), carried mainly by φ(a,w,p) and φ(w,p), φ(a,p), φ(a,w): every column containing `p` has its non-linear part supported on the 5 rows where p=1, and all 5 of them are negatives (0 of the 20 positives). S3 k=3 (smallest singular value 0.033) is full rank but weakly identified for the same reason.

**Identifiability of individual coefficients on these 198 rows** (full column rank is necessary, not sufficient; the condition number is in the table above):

| set | k=1 | k=2 | k=3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| S3 | yes | yes | yes, ill-conditioned |
| S5 | yes | no (structural redundancy 1) + finite-sample 1 | no (structural redundancy 4) + finite-sample 5 |
| S4 | yes | yes | no (finite-sample rank 12/14) |

Consequence: the main k-comparison uses S3. For S4 k=3 the coefficients φ(p,t), φ(a,p,t), φ(w,p,t) are not individually identified; S5 k=2 and k=3 contain structurally non-identified columns. Aggregate behaviour of the penalised model (predictions, validation metrics) is still well defined in every case, because L2/L1 select one solution; it is the individual coefficients that must not be read.

## 7. Interpretation

_Written by hand after inspecting the generated tables; kept in a separate file so the script stays reproducible. Conservative by design: 20 positives (4 per validation fold), weak features, 2026 snapshots. The shuffled-label runs are a descriptive control, not a statistical test._

**0. Feature sets and what can be interpreted (sections 2b-2c).**
- **S3 is the primary set.** It is the only set with full column rank at k=1,2,3 (3, 6, 7 coefficients), structurally and on the 198 rows. At k=3 it is weakly identified (smallest singular value 0.033, condition number 113) because every column containing `founder_previous_yc` is non-zero in only 5 rows, and all 5 of those rows are negatives.
- **S4** (extra set) is full rank at k=1,2 but rank 12 of 14 at k=3. The cause is finite-sample, not structural: exactly one row has `founder_previous_yc`=1 and `TLD_not_com`=1, and on all 198 rows min(a,p,t)=a*·min(p,t) and min(w,p,t)=w*·min(p,t) (a*, w* = that row's values). With all feature combinations observed (simulation) S4 is full rank at every k. Binary variables are what make this possible (min of binaries is a product), and the rare support of `founder_previous_yc` makes it happen here.
- **S5** (original brief) is structurally rank-deficient (mutually exclusive TLD dummies) plus finite-sample redundancy. It is kept as a sensitivity result; its TLD pair/triple coefficients are not identified and are not interpreted.

**1. Does the official pipeline work on W26?** Yes. The official basis matches an independent evaluation of Eq. (1) for k=1..3 (values and coalition-to-column mapping), k=1 equals MinMax + plain sklearn Logistic Regression, and the L2 / L1 / unregularised settings equal native sklearn (section 2). No convergence warnings in any condition.

**2. S3, L2 C=1: does larger k help?** Not measurably. Validation ROC 0.569 / 0.561 / 0.558 and PR-AUC 0.230 / 0.227 / 0.224 for k=1/2/3 (fold SD about 0.13 and 0.12), OOF Top-20 3.1 / 2.9 / 2.8. Training ROC is 0.603 at every k, so the extra columns do not even change the training fit under L2. The train−validation gap changes from 0.034 to 0.045. This says k=2 and k=3 add nothing detectable on S3; it does not say k=2 or k=3 are harmful, because S3 itself is barely above chance, so there is little signal for any k to improve.

**3. Is there evidence that k=3 mainly adds noise/variance?** No. Under L2 the single S3 three-way coefficient is small (−0.065 ± 0.069; mean |coef| 0.076 for real labels vs 0.168 for shuffled labels), so k=3 is essentially inert on S3, not noisy. Where order-3 coefficients are larger (S5, S4) they are about the size of the shuffled-label coefficients (S4: 0.143 vs 0.139), but S4 k=3 and S5 k=3 contain unidentified columns, so those magnitudes are partly decided by the penalty.

**4. Stability of interaction coefficients.** Sign consistency is high for most coefficients with real labels (S3 k=3: 0.84 for order 2, 0.80 for order 3) but the same statistic with shuffled labels is 0.85-0.88, so the 70% reference level is also reached with uninformative labels and does not separate signal from noise in this setting. Folds share 60-75% of their training rows, which raises all stability figures. In S3 the two pair coefficients with `founder_previous_yc` (n_founders ×: +0.204, sign consistency 1.00; one-liner words ×: −0.215, 0.98) rest on the 5 previous-YC rows, none of which is a positive; they carry the same information as the main effect "previous-YC rows are never positive" and I do not interpret them as interactions. Order-2 magnitudes in S3 are not larger than under shuffled labels (0.174 vs 0.193). I do not interpret any coefficient economically.

**5. Real vs shuffled labels (descriptive).**
- Theoretical chance is ROC-AUC 0.5 and Top-20 ≈ 2.0. The first 10 permutations gave fold validation ROC of 0.47-0.50, which I had described as "systematically below 0.5". That was an overreading of 10 permutations: a 100-permutation calibration (S3, L2) gives fold validation ROC 0.504 ± 0.009 (k=1) and 0.500 ± 0.009 (k=3), consistent with 0.5. The earlier 0.47-0.48 was sampling variability.
- The pooled OOF metrics on this fixed dataset do sit a little below their theoretical references under shuffling: OOF ROC 0.491 ± 0.008 / 0.484 ± 0.009 and OOF Top-20 1.63 ± 0.11 / 1.62 ± 0.12 (reference 2.0). With a fixed number of positives, positives in a training fold are absent from the validation fold, which can produce such a shift; I report both references and do not build a conclusion on the difference.
- S3 real ROC (0.56-0.57) is about 0.06-0.07 above the shuffled values and within the permutation SD (0.07-0.09 for the first 10 permutations; SD of the per-permutation mean over 100 permutations is about 0.09); the real-label S3 result is therefore not clearly separated from shuffled-label behaviour, consistent with the earlier Model A. S4 and S5 are higher than their shuffled runs (0.69-0.72 vs 0.50), but this comes from TLD, which we suspect reflects Google/domain measurement behaviour rather than startup quality.
- Shuffled-label training ROC rises with k (S5 0.641 → 0.678) and the train−validation gap under shuffled labels (0.13 in S3, 0.24-0.27 unregularised) is larger than with real labels (0.03-0.06 under L2), so the gap alone should not be read as a measure of how much signal a model has captured.

**6. Regularisation (secondary, one setting per penalty, no tuning).**
- **S3 (primary).** Real labels, validation ROC / PR-AUC / OOF ROC / OOF AP / Top-20 / gap / max |coef|:
  - k=2: L2 0.561 / 0.227 / 0.532 / 0.129 / 2.9 / 0.041 / 1.2; L1 0.456 / 0.102 / 0.443 / 0.093 / 1.1 / 0.090 / 1.7; unregularised 0.544 / 0.184 / 0.516 / 0.117 / 2.2 / 0.063 / 8.0.
  - k=3: L2 0.558 / 0.224 / 0.531 / 0.128 / 2.8 / 0.045 / 1.2; L1 0.456 / 0.102 / 0.443 / 0.093 / 1.1 / 0.090 / 1.7; unregularised 0.544 / 0.184 / 0.516 / 0.117 / 2.2 / 0.062 / 7.7.
  - L1 (C=1, saga) is almost intercept-only: 29 of 50 fits have all coefficients zero (ROC exactly 0.5, PR-AUC equal to prevalence 0.101), the others pick a main effect (never a pair or triple), and k=2 and k=3 are identical. L1 therefore "removes" higher-order terms only because at C=1 it removes nearly everything; with a single C I cannot say anything about L1 at other strengths.
  - Unregularised fits on S3 with real labels stay finite (max |coef| 8; mean |coef| of main effects 2.8 vs 0.40 under L2), with no convergence warnings. With shuffled labels they blow up (mean over permutations of the per-fit maximum: 152 at k=2, 844 at k=3). The validation differences between L2, unregularised and L1 are of the order of the fold SD (0.12-0.13).
- **S5 (sensitivity).** L2 0.711 / 0.713, unregularised 0.690 / 0.658 with max |coef| 9.1 / 1,963 and gap 0.11 / 0.15, L1 0.644 / 0.644 always selecting the redundant TLD_ai × TLD_other column. Behaviour here partly reflects how each penalty resolves redundant columns.
- Qualitatively consistent with the paper's argument that regularisation controls the growth in k: L2 keeps coefficients bounded and makes k=2 and k=3 behave like k=1; L1 at C=1 removes the high-order terms and most of the rest; no regularisation lets the high-order models become extreme when the data carry no signal or the basis is redundant. This does not show k=2 + L2 is the right model for startups.

**What this supports for discussion.** The official pipeline runs and was validated; on the well-posed S3 set higher k adds nothing measurable under L2; the shuffled-label control, the rank diagnostic and the identifiability table show how easily encoding choices and rare binary support create columns that cannot be interpreted. With the current features, nothing here is evidence about interactions.


## 8. Limits of this experiment

- 20 positives (4 per validation fold): every metric is wide; SDs across folds/repeats are not confidence intervals (repeats reuse the same 198 rows; training sets of different folds overlap).
- S3/S5 contain almost no signal (the earlier plain-LR Model A had OOF ROC 0.54, Top-20 3.3 vs chance 2.0). Three of the five features are (near-)binary and `founder_previous_yc` is 1 in only 5 of 193 rows, so most interaction columns are almost entirely zero.
- All features come from 2026 snapshots (caveat tier). The target is dominated by 11 article-covered startups plus 9 attention-driven positives; TLD is plausibly tied to Google/domain measurement.
- MinMax is fitted on training folds; validation values can fall outside [0,1] and nothing clips them (official behaviour).
- The shuffled-label control is descriptive, not inferential: 10 permutations, one dataset, overlapping folds. It shows what the metrics look like when labels carry no information; it gives no p-values or confidence intervals. The theoretical chance ROC-AUC is 0.5; deviations of the 10-permutation mean from 0.5 are treated as sampling variability.
